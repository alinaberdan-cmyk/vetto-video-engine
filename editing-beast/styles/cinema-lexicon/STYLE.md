# Cinema Lexicon

> A spoken idea (a quote, a podcast line, an interview answer, a poem) where **every phrase gets its own cinematic example clip** that shows literally what the words say. The type is small, white and minimal. Each video opens and closes on a **branded object or title card** (a ticket, a flip phone, a monitor, a card on silver), with one brand colour and one brand glyph. Sound design carries the cuts.

## 1. How to recognise when to use this style
Use Cinema Lexicon when the input is **a voice saying something reflective or motivational** (a quote, a podcast cut, an interview answer, a poem), 10–70 s, and the brief wants it to feel cinematic, profound or "corecore". The words matter more than the speaker's face.

**Signature (present in every reference):**
1. **One phrase → one clip.** A new shot on almost every phrase (median 1.3–1.5 s; up to ~3 s for a slow poem). The clip **literally illustrates** the phrase.
2. **Small white sans captions in the centre**, 1–3 words at a time, cut on the spoken word. Only key words get big.
3. **Branded bookends:** it opens on an object or title card in the brand colour and returns to it at the end.
4. **One brand colour (deep green) + one brand glyph (8-point star)**, used on titles, masks and end cards. Never on running captions.
5. **Wall-to-wall sound:** a continuous music bed under the voice, plus sound effects on cuts and graphics.

**Not this style:** a talking head with occasional B-roll, captions in boxes or karaoke highlights, emoji, the same clip held across several ideas, clips that only loosely match the words.

## 2. What stays fixed and what can vary
| Fixed | Range seen across refs |
|---|---|
| Phrase-level literal illustration | Footage source: famous films (ref1/3/4/5) ↔ the subject's own footage (ref6) |
| Centred small white captions, hard-cut on word onsets | Speaker on screen 0% (ref1, ref5) ↔ ~40% (ref4, ref6) |

| Continuous music bed + SFX | Graphics density: almost none (ref3, ref6) ↔ heavy (ref5 icons, bubbles, phone UI, glitch) |

## 3. Structure template
| Part | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | 0–3 s | The bookend. Either a **prop** (ticket swinging in 3D in front of the first clip; flip phone on white with the speaker on its screen; a desk or monitor shot with the clip playing on the screen) or a **title card**: the question in brand-green kinetic type on a silver radial ground, ending with the speaker appearing in a rounded card that grows to full frame. The first spoken words are already on screen. |
| **Body** | — | Phrase → clip, one after another. Return to the speaker for "I / you / we" statements and for reactions (~20–40% of shots when a speaker exists). Every 8–15 s, one **graphic beat**: a giant number, UI choice buttons, icons, a star-mask list, a phone frame. |
| **Build** | last 25% | A list or crescendo ("be. / think. / learn. / love.", "venal, petty, cruel, arbitrary", "food / water / hope") gets **one shot per word**, faster (0.6–1.0 s). |
| **Close** | last 2–4 s | Back to the bookend: the ticket held in hands; the monitor showing a CTA slide with the glyph; the phone flies off with motion blur; the card shrinks and slides out left on silver; or green tint → green gradient + star → the opening shot (seamless loop). |

## 4. Choosing the example clip (the core skill)
For each phrase, pick the clip with the **most literal reading** of its key word. Then align the clip's action peak to the moment that word is spoken.

| The phrase contains… | Show… | Seen |
|---|---|---|
| A concrete noun | That object or place | "a book" → man reading; "algorithms" → a hall of screens; "water" → water pouring; "kids" → children; "flip phone" → someone opening a flip phone |
| An action verb | Someone doing it | "observing sunsets" → silhouettes at sunset; "gazing into the night sky" → a figure facing the sea at dusk; "wander" → walking through a night market |
| An insult or comic image | A cartoon or meme frame | "monkey brains" → Simpsons monkey |
| Body or science | Scientific imagery | "your brain" → MRI slice |
| Abstract emotion or state | An iconic film moment with that emotion | "could exist" → Everything Everywhere multiverse faces; "hype" → American Psycho grin; "peace" → a man resting his head; "confident… success" → Christian Bale (The Big Short) arms crossed |
| A crowd or society idea | Wide crowd or consumer shots | "consumption stuff" → Apple-store crowd |
| Digital or social media | **Motion graphics over footage** | "the likes" → phone frame + platform icons + floating hearts; "comment section" → chat bubbles; "read a book or look at TikTok" → choice buttons |
| A number | **Giant typography** on white | "stupendous odds" → "1/400,000,000,000" scrolling |
| The speaker talks about themselves | The speaker's own clip | "I try to read", "I no longer use…" |

- Reframe wide films to 9:16 by centring on the subject. Keep each film's native grade. Light, contrasty clips and night scenes are both fine.
- **Never reuse a clip** within one video. Rhyming clips (the same actor, the same setting) are allowed when the phrase continues.
- **Rights:** the references use copyrighted film clips. **Example footage comes from Pexels** (free licence, no watermark), using the topic search for the brief (e.g. "food delivery"), plus the client's own footage. Envato Elements works too if a subscription exists. Never use watermarked previews in a final, and never remove watermarks.
- **Max 2 stock clips in a row.** Break longer runs with the creator's own footage or a brand graphic (CL01 review). If the topic search has no literal match for a phrase, use the creator's footage instead of an off-topic clip.
- **Search craft (Pexels/Envato):** search the *literal reading* plus a cinematic qualifier, e.g. "man reading book window light cinematic", "crowd shopping mall overhead", "silhouette father daughter sunset", "mri brain scan", "water pouring slow motion". Prefer clips that are cinematic, shallow-focus, graded and 4K, and that reframe well to 9:16 (subject centred). Avoid obvious corporate stock (smiling handshakes, white-background offices).

## 5. Typography
### 5.1 Running caption
- Font: **neo-grotesk bold** (Helvetica Neue Bold / Neue Haas). Stand-in: **Inter 700**, tracking −0.02em.
- Size: **3.6–4.5% of width → ~40–48 px at 1080w**, lowercase as spoken, punctuation kept ("sunsets.", "with.", "so…").
- Position: **horizontally centred, vertically 45–55%** (on the subject's chest or the scene centre). ref1 also uses the top third (~20%) on wide shots. Keep the position from shot to shot.
- Timing: **hard cut on the spoken onset, no animation** (verified at 30 fps). 1–3 words, replaced chunk by chunk. A chunk clears on the cut that ends its phrase.
- Fill: white, with a **soft vertical gradient tinted from the scene** (the top ~100% white; the bottom picks up the shot's dominant highlight hue at ~25%). Examples: "confident" white→rose, "you're breathing" pale blue→orange. No stroke, no box. A faint shadow only on very bright shots.

### 5.2 Key word (emphasis)
- The same family, **8–14% of width (~90–150 px)**. Used on 1 word in ~4: "DO.", "can't.", "experience", "peace.", "else.", "sideline", "hype".
- Variants: **italic** for the stressed word ("will *never*", "do *all* you can", "*a book*", "*risk*", "*one thing*", "*any moment*"); **ALL CAPS** for shouts ("BAD MEAL", "HOPE", "F\*CK YOU", "CONSUMPTION STUFF").
- **Hanging stack:** small connector words (35–45% of the key word's size) hang off a corner of the key word: "will *never* / **experience** / that", "be open to / **experience**", "**human** / race / as a whole", "those / who / **can't.**".
- Large caps over footage use an **overlay-style blend** (~80–90%), so the scene's texture shows through ("CONSUMPTION STUFF", "F\*CK YOU", "compete").
- **Scene-coloured words:** on strongly graded shots, the whole word takes the scene colour (orange "observing" on a sunset, blue "gazing into" at dusk).
- **In-scene text:** occasionally the text is placed **on a surface in the shot** and coloured to match: "the stupidest person in the room" on a wall hanging in warm gold, "take away your screen." on a desk mat. Or it sits **behind an object** (the ticket covers "you").

### 5.3 Brand typography (bookends and graphic beats only)
- Deep green **#007A4E** bold sans on white **#FDFDFD** or on the silver radial ground (**#FCFCFC** centre → **#8F8F8F** edges, the light slowly breathing).
- Mixed with a green **formal script** for one word per line ("*learn*", "*travels?*", "*away?*"). Stand-in: Pinyon Script / Great Vibes.
- Kinetic layout: words fly in one by one, sizes jump (small "from all" → huge "YOUR" → script "travels?"), then the line slides off sideways with motion blur.
- Bold italic caps for punchlines on product shots ("to **CARRY** a **FLIP-PHONE**").
- CTA slide: green caps on white with the glyph above ("OR CAN YOU STEP UP?").

## 6. Motion graphics and design elements
| Element | Spec | Seen |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand glyph** | 8-point starburst, green #1E9648 / mint #3CFCA8. Used as a logo on props, as a **mask window** (the clip plays inside the star, the star rotates slowly and scales up 0.5–0.7 s to become the next shot), and as an end card on an **animated green mesh gradient** (#BAE4C0 → #1E9648 → #3CFCA8 drifting) | ref1 list "be. think. learn. love.", ref3 CTA, ref5 end |
| **Hero prop** | A real-looking 3D object (paper ticket, flip phone cut-out, monitor, desk mat). It tilts and rotates in perspective, is tracked onto the footage (the ticket over a face, then held in hands), and has its **screen replaced** by footage (phone, monitor) | ref1, ref3, ref4, ref5 |
| **Rounded card** | The speaker clip as a rounded rectangle on silver, which grows to full frame (open) or shrinks and exits left (close) | ref6 |
| **Giant number** | Green bold numerals 40–60% of the frame height on white, scrolling horizontally with motion blur | ref1 "1/400,000,000,000" |
| **UI choice buttons** | Rounded rectangles, 2 px white outline, white bold caps ("READ A BOOK"); the chosen one fills with brand green ("LOOK AT TIKTOK"), with "or" between | ref4 |
| **Platform icons** | Real app icons (Instagram, TikTok, X, Reddit, Facebook, Pinterest) in a row; they pop in with overshoot and swap as a set | ref5 |
| **Floating hearts** | Pink notification hearts rising and fading above the subject | ref5 |
| **Chat bubbles** | Small teal rounded bubbles with tiny text, popping in around the subject | ref5 "comment section" |
| **Phone frame** | A thin white phone outline with a home indicator around the footage | ref5 |
| **Brand tint** | One clip near the end recoloured to a **brand-green duotone**, leading into the end card | ref5 "focus on yourself" |

## 7. Transitions
- **Hard cuts** on phrase boundaries: ~90%.
- **Into or out of a screen:** the clip lives on a monitor or phone screen, then cuts to full frame, and back at the end.
- **Card grow / shrink** on silver (open / close).
- **Glyph mask:** the star window grows to fill the frame and becomes the next shot.
- **Object exit:** the prop flies off with heavy motion blur (end).
- **Brand-green dissolve:** green tint → gradient + star → crossfade to the opening shot (loop).
- Blur-swap on white title cards (word out with blur, next in).

## 8. Sound design
*(Measured from the files, not heard. Verify by ear on the next pass.)*
- **The voice is the clock and stays dominant.**
- **A continuous music bed in every reference:** the quietest 10% of each track still sits at −24…−28 dB (−36 dB in the poem), so the bed never stops. It is cinematic and emotional, mixed ~8–12 dB under the voice.
- **SFX on cuts:** ref4 has high-frequency swish bursts on 21 of 22 cuts. ref1 has low "boom" accents on ~13 of 22 cuts (emphasis cuts and number beats).
- **Diegetic film audio** briefly surfaces on reaction shots (a scream, a laugh) under the voice.
- **Team SFX pack mapping** (`sfx-library/celf-sfx-pack/`):
  | Event | Effect |
  |---|---|
  | Phrase cuts (rotate 2–3 takes, quiet) | `Whoosh_05_Fast_Flick`, `Whoosh_02_Wave`, `Whoosh_01_Sway` |
  | Big number or title slam | `Whoosh_03_Reverse_Rise` into the hit |
  | Icons, bubbles, hearts | `Pop_01_Tap`, `Pop_02_Quick` |
  | Choice-button select | `Mouse_Click_02_Tactile` or `UI_01_Tiny_Bleep` |
  | CTA on a monitor | `Typing_03_Short_Phrase` |
  | Prop flip, ticket snap | `Camera_02_Shutter_Click` |
  | Star end card | `Sparkle_04_Scene_Reveal` (once) |

## 9. Build checklist (per new video)
1. Transcribe with word timestamps. Split into phrases (cut points).
2. Write a **clip list**: one literal example per phrase (table in §4), with the in-point at the action peak. Mark the "I/you" phrases for the speaker's own clip.
3. Choose the bookend (prop or title card) and the brand colour and glyph.
4. Plan 1–3 graphic beats (number, buttons, icons, star-mask list).
5. Captions: centred running captions on onsets. Pick key words for big, italic or caps treatments and hanging stacks.
6. Sound: music bed + SFX map. Voice on top.
7. Render, then check every cut lands on a phrase boundary and every clip reads at a glance.

## 10. Learned from productions
**CL01: feedmeplzthanks UGC app ad (creator holding the phone, 39.6 s), approved v6, 2026-11-17**
- **The brand comes from the product itself.** Pull the icon, screenshots, preview video and colours from the App Store listing (see `projects/cl01/BRAND.md`). Here: cream `#FCF0D4`, magenta `#E028B0`, the winking-face icon as the glyph, and Lilita One as a stand-in for the app's chunky display face. These replace the reference green and star.
- **Product UI beats product B-roll.** When a phrase names a feature ("food categories", "options", "surprise button", "swipe left/right"), show the product's *real* UI rebuilt as graphics: category pills with the same colours, black outlines, offset shadows and icons that **drop from the top and bounce into a pile over the creator's blurred take**; a "Surprise me" pill; ✕ / ♥ swipe chips; the official screen recording inside a phone frame.
- **Example clips must act out the human meaning, not just the topic.** "Showed it to me" → two people scrolling one phone. "Fun" → friends laughing at dinner. "Take forever → what to eat" → someone thinking, then someone torn between pizza and a burger.
- **Never more than 2 stock clips in a row**, and fewer is fine. Where the creator is demoing the product, keep her footage instead of stock (v6 has 9 stock clips in 39 s).
- **Stock source:** Pexels (free, no watermark), with a per-phrase search. Envato previews are watermarked and the watermark is never removed.
- **Bookends for a UGC ad:** open on a cream card ("*dare* / you to try / THIS APP." + the app icon + her phone + category pills). Close by shrinking her take into a rounded card under the icon and app name, with chat bubbles, "what you think." and the slogan "Stop deciding. Dinner's sorted."
- **Captions:** use the "low" position (under the phone) whenever the app screen is visible, "mid" on stock, and dark text on cream cards.

**CL02: CEO on competitors, two-clip interview (31.6 s), approved v2, 2026-11-18**
- **The no-pictures variant.** When the brief says "no pictures", use only the people's own footage. The style then comes from word-timed captions (big italic or caps key words; brand name in Lilita magenta with a white stroke), hard punch-ins on the key words (LEFT/RIGHT shift sideways, a growing zoom on approve ×3, a big zoom on "none."), and the cream card shrink/grow as the only transition.
- **Several clips:** the shorter clip goes first. Music sits only under the part she asks for (here part 2: "Sneaky Snitch", Kevin MacLeod, CC BY 4.0, at ~−34 dB under the voice). The intro stays dry.
- **Get to the point.** Cut the setup chatter, the re-framing pauses, and any interviewer question that repeats the intro. Alina removed "so I'm going to ask you about the competitors": the intro already set it up, so the cut goes straight into the answer.
- **No promo end card on interviews or opinion pieces.** End on the punchline ("literally none.") and fade the music. The branded close card is for ads only.
- **Captions on a seated person** go at chest height (y≈1280), never over the face.
- **Phone footage:** render with `--sdr` (see INDEX).
